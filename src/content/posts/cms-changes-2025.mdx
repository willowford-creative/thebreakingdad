If you're a separated parent in the UK, there are significant CMS changes coming to [the Child Maintenance Service](/separated-parents/child-maintenance/)...and they could affect how much child maintenance you pay or receive, how quickly payments are enforced, and what financial support you get.

The government has announced on 23rd June that a new set of reforms that aim to improve how child maintenance is managed, enforced, and transferred.

Here's everything you need to know in plain English, including when the changes will take place and what they mean for families like yours.

*Note: Where statistics are referenced, they are mostly pulled from the Government Consultation outcome document referenced above, unless otherwise stated.*

## When Do the CMS Changes Start?

OK, so there's good news and bad news.

We'll start with the bad news: The CMS referred to in this article won't come into force until **2027 at the earliest** as they require new legislation and system updates.

I've spoken to hundreds, if not thousands of separated parents over the years and I know reform can't come soon enough for so many people.

So unfortunately, before we go any further, I thought it'd be grounding the article in reality. For now at least, whether you're using **Direct Pay** or the **Collect and Pay service,** the same process will remain in place.

## Why Are These CMS Changes Happening?

As I mentioned above, it's clear that the Child Maintenance Service is not without its issues.

In fact, the statistics paint a clear picture:

- Only **60%** of Direct Pay users receive the full child maintenance they’re owed from the other parent
- Just **40%** get payments on time

With over **750,000 families** using the CMS, these failures in the child maintenance arrangement have had, and continue to have, a real impact on the lives of children.

## What Are the Main CMS Changes?

### 1. Faster Enforcement After Missed Payments

CMS will now be able to take action **after just one missed payment**, helping reduce arrears and make enforcement more effective from day one.

### 2. Automatic Deductions from Bank Accounts

CMS will gain the power to **automatically deduct payments from a paying parent’s bank account**, without requiring a court order.

### 3. A New Service Structure

Direct Pay and Collect and Pay will merge into a **single monitored service**, offering improved consistency and oversight across all cases.

### 4. New Fees for Everyone

A **2% fee** will be applied to **both paying and receiving parents**, even those who previously used fee-free Direct Pay:

- Paying parent: pays 102%
- Receiving parent: receives 98%

The **20% surcharge** for enforcement remains.

## Direct Pay Changes and What They Mean for Families

Direct Pay users will now face fees for the first time. This will impact families who previously chose this option to avoid charges. While the government argues the fees fund better enforcement, many parents will need to factor this into future budgets.

## How CMS Changes Affect Paying Parents

Paying parents will see:

- Enforcement after one missed payment
- No need for court orders to deduct payments
- A 2% service charge
- Increased monitoring and accountability

## Collect and Pay Service Transformation

The new system will replace the existing Collect and Pay option, bringing:

- Fewer service distinctions
- Consistent enforcement rules
- Professional oversight
- The 2% fee (replacing Collect and Pay’s previous fees)

## CMS Support for Domestic Abuse Survivors

Previously, survivors could avoid Collect and Pay to reduce contact. These new reforms may limit that flexibility.

The government has promised **safeguards** for vulnerable parents who have experienced domestic abuse, and organisations like [Gingerbread](https://www.gingerbread.org.uk/our-work/news-and-views/what-do-the-cms-changes-mean-for-me/) are calling for **trauma-informed processes**.

## Family-Based Arrangements and the Transition to CMS

Private arrangements will still be possible, but the CMS will encourage more families to transition to the official system. The new structure promises better enforcement, but also introduces fees, so families should weigh up whether professional oversight is worth the cost.

## CMS Changes in Northern Ireland

Northern Ireland runs its own CMS, but it often aligns with UK-wide changes. Parents should monitor updates from NI Direct for confirmation of how reforms may be mirrored locally.

## Addressing Child Poverty Through Better Enforcement

Irregular child maintenance contributes to child poverty. The government hopes better enforcement will lead to more consistent payments, easing pressure on single-parent households.  
([Source: Parliament Briefing](https://researchbriefings.files.parliament.uk/documents/CBP-9661/CBP-9661.pdf))

## Will the CMS Recalculate Maintenance Payments?

Yes, a future consultation will review how maintenance is calculated, which could impact:

- Self-employed parents
- Families receiving benefits
- Parents with irregular incomes

([Source: The Independent](https://www.independent.co.uk/news/uk/home-news/child-maintenance-changes-dwp-rates-direct-pay-collect-b2776829.html))

## What Campaigners Are Calling For

Charities like Gingerbread are asking for:

- A child poverty-first approach
- Clearer communication and updates
- Post-enforcement support
- Adequate CMS staffing and digital systems
- Greater transparency and accountability

## Key Proposals Summary

- **2027 implementation** – no changes yet
- **One missed payment** triggers enforcement
- **Unified service** replaces Direct Pay and Collect and Pay
- **2% fees** apply to all
- **Bank deductions** without court involvement
- **Calculation review** planned for later stages
- **Domestic abuse protections** still being developed

## Final Thoughts

Child maintenance isn’t a bonus, it’s a basic need. These reforms could mark a real shift in how the CMS supports parents and children across the UK.

For many, it means more predictable support. For others, the changes bring new costs and concerns. Either way, knowing what’s coming helps you prepare.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When do the CMS changes take effect?

The changes to the Child Maintenance Service are expected to come into force in 2027 at the earliest, following new legislation and a system rollout.

### **Will I have to pay fees if I use Direct Pay for CMS?**

Yes, under the new CMS system, all parents—whether using Direct Pay or not—will be charged a 2% fee on payments.

### **What happens if a parent misses a CMS payment?**

The CMS will be able to take enforcement action after just one missed payment, including direct deductions from bank accounts.

### **Can I still make a private child maintenance arrangement?**

Family-based arrangements will still be possible, but the new CMS structure encourages monitored payments with improved enforcement.

### **Will the way child maintenance is calculated change?**

Yes, the government plans to consult on how maintenance is calculated in a future stage of reform, which may affect self-employed and variable-income parents.
