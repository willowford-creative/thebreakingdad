## **Brewing with a Cafetière: A Classic Start**

I started with the method I knew best: the trusty cafetière (or French press). Pact suggested a coarse grind, so I set the Sage grinder accordingly. Here’s what I did:

- 15g of coffee (about a tablespoon and a half)
- 250ml of hot water (just off the boil)
- Stir, steep for 4 minutes, plunge slowly

The result? A smooth, rich cup with subtle chocolate notes. It had a natural sweetness—no bitterness, no burnt taste, just a clean, comforting brew.

Compared to my usual pre-ground coffee, the difference was night and day. It felt like a proper café-style coffee, made in my own kitchen.

Other brewing methods like espresso machines also offer versatile options depending on personal preferences and the type of coffee beans available.

### What I've learned after a few weeks

As I explained at the beginning of this post, I was a complete coffee novice. I didn't really know where to start. I've experimented a little and, using a grinder, I found it a little tricky to predictably gauge how much coffee I needed compared to water. I did some research and found the following ratios:  
  
**1g coffee to 15ml water for a strong coffee**  
**1g coffee to 17ml water for a medium coffee**  
  
To make it more consistent, I created this chart to help lock down a consistent flavour.   
  
The other huge factor I discovered is a process known as, 'the bloom'. This is where you pouring a small amount of hot water over fresh coffee grounds and let it sit sit for 30 seconds. This allows trapped carbon dioxide gas to escape, resulting in a better-tasting, more flavorful cup of coffee.

## **Trying the V60: My First Pour-Over Experience**

The V60 was completely new to me, but since Pact included one in my order, I gave it a go. This method requires a finer grind than a cafetière, so I adjusted the grinder. Here’s how I brewed it:

- One scoop of coffee (included in the pack)
- Approximately 250ml of water, poured in slow, circular motions
- Brew time: About 2.5 minutes

At first, I found the process a bit fiddly, but once I got into the rhythm, it felt quite meditative.

The result? A noticeably lighter, more delicate coffee. The chocolate notes were still there, but with more clarity and a smoother texture. If the cafetière brew was bold and full-bodied, this was like its more refined cousin—clean, bright, and incredibly drinkable.

This method also allows you to explore different coffees, experiencing a range of flavors from various origins and roasts.
